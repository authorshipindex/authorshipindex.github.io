Why it is separate from the standard

Writing tools acquire and lose generative features every few months. A standard that names products is out of date the day it publishes, and reissuing a standard to reclassify one piece of software would be absurd.

So the standard carries the principle — did the tool have to understand your work, and did it write any words that survived — and this page carries the product-level answers. The principle is stable. The answers are not.

What a ruling will look like

A named tool or practice, the number it lands on, and the reasoning in one or two sentences — tied back to the two tests, never to a private judgement about the product.

Where a tool has changed behaviour, the previous ruling stays visible with its date. A declaration made in good faith under an old ruling was made in good faith.

Until then

The two questions in the standard settle most cases without a ruling. Did the tool have to understand your work to do its job? If yes, you are at least a 1. Did it write any of the words that survived? If yes, at least a 4.
